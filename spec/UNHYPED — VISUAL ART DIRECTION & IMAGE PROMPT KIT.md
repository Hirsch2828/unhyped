## UNHYPED — VISUAL ART DIRECTION & IMAGE PROMPT KIT  
## 1. Visual Backbone — עמוד השדרה הוויזואלי  
**Calm editorial intelligence in a world of noisy technology news.**  
Unhyped should visually feel like a premium morning publication crossed with a modern intelligence product: warm, spacious, trustworthy and exceptionally easy to scan.  
The visual world should communicate:  
**“The internet is noisy. This is where the noise stops.”**  
It should NOT feel like:  
* futuristic AI software  
* a crypto dashboard  
* a stock terminal  
* an RSS reader  
* a social-media feed  
* a generic SaaS dashboard  
* a neon cyberpunk interface  
The visual tension should always be:  
**chaos outside → clarity inside**

**#C86F4A**

**#D6423A**

**Never use Breaking Red decoratively.**  
Its rarity is what gives it power.

## How to Extend the Style  
For additional illustrations, keep the same palette, texture, geometry and editorial restraint, and replace only the central metaphor.  
Examples:  
* duplicate stories → many cards becoming one  
* reliability → unstable fragments becoming a solid shape  
* personalization → one stream splitting into individually relevant paths  
* daily brief → many scattered items reduced to five  
* update → one existing card receiving a small new layer  
* trending → controlled directional movement rather than flames or hype graphics  
* rumor → intentionally less solid / less resolved geometry  
* official → stable and complete geometry  
  
## F. TYPOGRAPHY  
## HEADLINES — MANROPE  
**English: Manrope**  
Why: contemporary and highly readable, but slightly warmer and more editorial than many standard SaaS grotesks. Its clean geometry works well with the understated Unhyped identity.  
Recommended usage:  
* Hero headlines  
* section titles  
* story titles  
* large numeric information  
* buttons sparingly  
Weights: **600 / 700**  
## Hebrew counterpart: HEEBO  
Use **Heebo SemiBold / Bold** for Hebrew headlines.  
It maintains a modern geometric feel while remaining highly readable in Hebrew interfaces.  
  
## BODY — INTER  
**English: Inter**  
Why: optimized for interface reading, highly legible at small sizes and neutral enough to let the editorial content rather than the font become the focus.  
Recommended usage:  
* summaries  
* metadata  
* source information  
* body copy  
* settings  
* controls  
Weights: **400 / 500 / 600**  
## Hebrew counterpart: NOTO SANS HEBREW  
Use **Noto Sans Hebrew Regular / Medium** for longer Hebrew text.  
It is exceptionally readable, neutral and dependable for content-heavy screens.  
  
## TYPOGRAPHIC PERSONALITY  
The combination should feel like:  
**Editorial intelligence, not startup branding.**  
Use:  
* large headlines  
* short line lengths  
* generous line spacing  
* strong hierarchy  
* limited font weights  
Avoid:  
* all caps everywhere  
* compressed headlines  
* tiny metadata  
* aggressive bold typography  
* “AI futuristic” fonts  
* monospace as a primary brand font

Physical, editorial visual metaphor rather than stock-market cliché.  
## Rumor  
More ambiguous, partially obscured visual composition.  
## Official  
Stable, clean and confident composition.  
  
## H. BREAKING VISUAL RULE  
Breaking must visually feel unusual.  
Use:  
**Breaking Red #D6423A**  
only for:  
* small Breaking badge  
* tiny accent line  
* occasional status dot  
Do NOT:  
* turn the whole card red  
* use flashing effects  
* add warning triangles  
* make the product feel like cable news  
The point is:  
**If red appears, the user immediately notices because almost nothing else is red.**  
  
## I. CORE VISUAL PRINCIPLE  
Every design decision should pass one test:  
**Does this make the information feel clearer, calmer and more trustworthy?**  
If an element is visually impressive but increases cognitive load, remove it.  
Unhyped should visually embody the product promise:  
**Less information. More understanding.**
